What do we own at the end of all this?

Pakistan's tech scene has a well-worn path. Open a software house, find clients on Upwork, build their projects, invoice, repeat. Hundreds of companies run that playbook and it works. But Faizan kept asking a question that didn't have a good answer: what do we actually own at the end of all this?

That question turned into Avennex.

The idea was straightforward. Stop building other people's products and start building our own. Pick real problems, write the software, own the revenue. AI was the obvious focus because that's where the team's depth sits, and because the problems worth solving right now are drowning in manual work machines handle better.

Two products are in active development. An HR platform that screens hundreds of resumes before a recruiter opens their laptop. A storefront builder priced for sellers in Lahore and Dhaka, not enterprise teams in San Francisco. Both came from frustrations the team hit firsthand: our own hiring process was broken, and we watched local sellers pay $39 a month for tools that don't support local payment gateways.

Honestly, we're still figuring a lot of this out. No playbook exists for a product studio in Lahore that refuses outside money and runs on part-time schedules. Some weeks feel like real momentum. Others feel like pushing a wall.

Before any of this, the team shipped mobile apps and games to the Play Store. Those taught us how to build, test and release software real people download. The apps were the training ground. The products are the mission. Everyone works part-time alongside a day job, which isn't a footnote, it's just how a company runs when the founders refuse to take money before the product earns it.

How we work.

Own what you build

Client work pays bills but doesn't build a company. Every product in the pipeline is ours, from the first commit to the revenue it generates.

Less is more code

No architecture astronautics. If a feature ships in 200 lines, it ships in 200 lines. The codebase stays small enough that anyone on the team can read all of it.

Ship, then polish

A working version in someone's hands beats a perfect version in a planning doc. Release early, watch how people use it, fix what matters.
