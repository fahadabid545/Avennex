About Us

Built by builders

A product studio that owns what it ships. No clients, no outsourcing, no handoffs.

Pakistan's tech scene has a well-worn path. Open a software house, find clients on Upwork, build their projects, invoice, repeat. Hundreds of companies run that playbook. It works. But Faizan kept asking a question that didn't have a good answer: what do we actually own at the end of all this?

That question turned into Avennex.

The idea was straightforward. Stop building other people's products and start building our own. Pick real problems, write the software, own the revenue. AI was the obvious focus because that's where the team's depth sits, and because the problems worth solving right now are drowning in manual work that machines handle better.

Two products are in active development. An HR automation platform that screens hundreds of resumes before your recruiter opens their laptop. An e-commerce builder priced for small sellers in Lahore and Dhaka, not enterprise teams in San Francisco. Both came from frustrations the team hit firsthand. The HR tool started because our own hiring process was broken. The store builder came from watching local sellers pay $39/month for tools that don't even support local payment gateways.

Before all of this, the team shipped mobile apps and games to the Play Store. Those projects taught us how to build, test, and release software that real people download. The apps were the training ground. The products are the actual mission. Everyone on the team works part-time alongside day jobs. That's not a footnote or a disclaimer. It's just how early-stage companies run when the founders refuse to take money before the product earns it.

How we work

Own what you build

Client work pays the bills but doesn't build a company. Every product in our pipeline is ours, from the first commit to the revenue it generates.

Less is more code

No architecture astronautics. If a feature can ship with 200 lines, it ships with 200 lines. The codebase stays small enough that anyone on the team can read all of it.

Ship, then polish

A working version in someone's hands beats a perfect version in a planning doc. Release early, watch how people use it, fix what matters.
