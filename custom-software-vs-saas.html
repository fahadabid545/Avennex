Custom software or off the shelf?

A plain comparison for teams deciding whether to buy a subscription or build something of their own.

The short answer.

Off the shelf software is the faster and cheaper start when your process looks like everyone else's. Custom software makes sense when the way you work is part of how you make money, or when your team loses hours every week working around the tools it has.

Side by side.

Which one fits?

When off the shelf is the better pick

  • Your process is standard, like payroll, bookkeeping or email.
  • You need it running this week.
  • One tool already covers most of the job and the budget is tight.
  • Nobody on your side will look after the software once it's live.

When custom software pays off

  • Workarounds and spreadsheets eat hours every week.
  • Several tools are held together by copy and paste.
  • The way you work is what sets you apart.
  • Seat fees climb with every hire.
  • Your data has to live in a specific place.

The middle path.

Most businesses end up with both. The bought tools keep doing the standard work, and a smaller custom piece covers what they miss, connected to them through their APIs.

That's often the smallest build and the quickest win. It's also the first option on the table in a scoping call.

Cost over time.

Compare three years, not the first month. For the subscription, add seats, add ons, price changes and the hours your team spends on workarounds. For custom, add the build, hosting and upkeep.

Put both totals side by side. The answer is usually obvious once the hidden hours are counted.

Common questions.

Is custom software more expensive than SaaS?

At the start, yes. Over a few years it can cost less, especially when seat fees grow with your team or workarounds cost staff time. Comparing totals over three years shows which side wins for you.

Can custom software work with the tools we already use?

Yes, as long as those tools offer an API or an export. Connecting to what you already pay for is often the cheapest way to fill a gap.

Do we have to replace everything at once?

No. Most projects start with one process that hurts the most, then grow release by release.
