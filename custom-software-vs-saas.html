Custom software or off the shelf?

A plain comparison for teams deciding whether to buy a subscription or build something of their own.

Buy or build, in short.

Off the shelf software is the faster and cheaper start when your process looks like everyone else's. Custom software makes sense when the way you work is part of how you make money, or when your team loses hours every week working around the tools it has.

SaaS vs custom software.

QuestionOff the shelf SaaSCustom software
Upfront costLow. Usually a monthly or yearly subscription.Higher. A one time build, priced after scoping.
Ongoing costPer seat or per tier fees that grow with your team.Hosting and upkeep, which don't rise with every hire.
Time to startDays, sometimes hours.Longer. The first working release is agreed in the plan.
Fit to your processYour team adapts to the tool.The tool follows how your team works.
ChangesRequests go on the vendor's roadmap.Changes are planned with you and released on your schedule.
DataStored where the vendor decides.Hosted where you decide.
Vendor riskPrice rises, feature cuts and shutdowns are out of your hands.Fewer outside decisions can change how it works.

When to buy, when to build.

When off the shelf is the better pick

  • Your process is standard, like payroll, bookkeeping or email.
  • You need it running this week.
  • One tool already covers most of the job and the budget is tight.
  • Nobody on your side will look after the software once it's live.

When custom software pays off

  • Workarounds and spreadsheets eat hours every week.
  • No mix of tools covers the process, even connected.
  • The way you work is what sets you apart.
  • Seat fees climb with every hire.
  • Your data has to live in a specific place.

Buy most of it, build the gap.

Plenty of businesses end up with both. Bought tools keep doing the standard work. A smaller custom piece, or a Zapier or Make flow, covers what they miss through their APIs.

It's usually the smallest build, and it's one of the first options laid out in a scoping call.

Three year cost, side by side.

Compare three years, not the first month. For the subscription, add seats, add ons, price changes and the hours your team spends on workarounds. For custom, add the build, hosting and upkeep.

Put both totals side by side, hidden hours included.

Custom software vs SaaS FAQ.

Is custom software more expensive than SaaS?

At the start, yes. Over a few years it can cost less, especially when seat fees grow with your team or workarounds cost staff time. Comparing totals over three years shows which side wins for you.

Can custom software work with the tools we already use?

Yes, as long as those tools offer an API or an export. Connecting them is often the cheapest way to fill a gap.

Do we have to replace everything at once?

No. Most projects start with one process that hurts the most, then grow release by release.

Buy, build or both?

Describe the problem. You'll get the options, including the ones that don't need us.
