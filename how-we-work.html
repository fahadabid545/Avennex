Let's talk

How we work

From first call to working software.

There's no price list here. Each project is scoped and priced around your problem, your budget and your timeline, once we both agree on the plan.

How a project runs.

1. Your problem, in detail

The first conversation is about your team's day. What's slowing people down, who feels it most, and what you've already tried.

2. The ways to solve it

There's usually more than one answer. A new build, a Zapier or Make flow between two systems, an addition to software you already run, or a setting nobody switched on. For each one you'll see what it takes, in people and in time.

3. What fits for you

Then it's your turn. Tell us the budget you have in mind, the date that matters and anything you'd rather leave out. The scope gets shaped around your answers.

4. One plan, one agreed price

Once the solution is settled, you get it in writing with scope, timeline and cost. The number comes after the plan, so it reflects your project and nothing else.

5. Short releases

Work arrives in small pieces your team can use and test. Feedback on each one shapes the next.

6. After launch

Live software still needs care. Updates, fixes and new features carry on for as long as you want them.

Why there's no price list.

Two projects with the same name can cost very different amounts. A client portal for 20 staff with one kind of login is a smaller job than one that talks to your accounting system and serves thousands of customers.

Pricing happens after scoping. You'll know what you're paying for and why, before any work starts.

What usually moves the cost

  • How many people and roles use it
  • Systems it has to connect to
  • Data that needs moving out of old tools
  • How much design the screens need
  • AI features that need testing on your own data
  • Support after launch

Pricing and process FAQ.

How much does custom software cost?

It depends on scope, which is why the price comes after the plan. The list above covers what usually moves it. Share a rough budget early and you'll see what fits inside it.

How long does a project take?

The timeline is agreed in the plan, next to the price. Larger projects are split into releases, so something works well before the whole thing is done.

What should I bring to the first call?

A description of the problem and who it affects. The tools your team uses today. A budget and a date if you have them. Screenshots of the spreadsheet everyone is tired of help too.

What if we only need part of it?

That's common. Tell us which parts matter most and the plan starts there. The rest can wait for a later release, or never happen at all.

Do you build on Zapier and Make, or only in code?

Both. Zapier, Make and custom code are all on the table, and the plan says which one each part uses and why.

Start with the problem.

Bring the problem and a rough budget if you have one. The plan and the price come after.
