Zapier, Make or custom automation?

When a no code builder is enough, and when a workflow deserves its own code.

The short answer.

Zapier and Make are a good start for connecting apps with simple rules. Custom automation earns its place when workflows get long, run at high volume, need logic the builders can't express cleanly, or carry data you want tight control over.

Side by side.

Which one fits?

When a no code builder is enough

  • The workflow has a handful of steps.
  • It runs a few hundred times a month, not thousands.
  • You're testing whether an idea works at all.
  • Someone on your team likes owning it.

When custom automation is worth it

  • The monthly bill keeps climbing with volume.
  • Flows break quietly and nobody notices for days.
  • The logic needs branching, lookups or AI steps.
  • Sensitive data shouldn't pass through another platform.

The middle path.

You don't have to pick one. Simple zaps can stay where they are while the heavy, high volume flow moves to code.

Another common path is to prove an idea in a builder first, then rebuild it properly once it's clearly worth keeping. Our workflow automation work often starts exactly there.

Cost over time.

Look at the bill at the volume you expect in a year, not today's. Usage based pricing is cheap for small flows and grows with every task.

Then add the time spent fixing broken runs. A custom workflow costs more on day one and stays flat as volume grows.

Common questions.

Is Zapier enough for a small business?

Often, yes. For short workflows at low volume it's quick and affordable. It starts to strain when flows get long, volume grows or failures go unnoticed.

When should we move off Zapier or Make?

When the bill rises faster than the business, when one broken step costs real money, or when the logic no longer fits on one screen.

Can AI steps be part of an automation?

Yes. Reading documents, sorting messages and drafting replies can all be steps in a workflow. Custom code makes it easier to test those steps on your own data before anyone relies on them.
