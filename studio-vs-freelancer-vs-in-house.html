Who should build your software?

A freelancer, an in house hire, a large agency or a software studio. What each one is good at.

Studio, freelancer or in house, in short.

A freelancer suits small, well defined tasks. An in house team makes sense when software is your core product for years. A large agency fits big programs with big budgets. A studio sits between them: a full team for a project, without hiring one.

Matching the option to the job.

When someone else is the better pick

  • Freelancer: the task is small, the spec is clear and you can review the work yourself.
  • In house: the software is what you sell and needs daily attention for years.
  • Large agency: the program spans many teams, countries or a long procurement process.

When a studio fits

  • You need more than one skill, like design plus AI plus integrations.
  • Hiring a team would take longer than the project.
  • You want one plan, one price and one point of contact.
  • The software needs care after launch, without a full time hire.

Mixing them.

These aren't exclusive. A studio can build the first version while you hire, then hand over to your in house team with documentation, on terms agreed at the start.

Some companies keep an internal team for daily changes and bring in a studio for bigger pieces of work. Here's how a project runs with us.

Rate vs total cost.

Compare the total for the whole project, not the hourly rate. A lower rate with more hours, or a salary for a role you only need for six months, often costs more in the end.

Count the time you'd spend managing the work as well. That cost is real even though it never shows up on an invoice.

Hiring vs studio FAQ.

Should a small business hire a developer or work with a studio?

If the software is your product and needs daily work, hiring makes sense. For a defined project, or one that needs several skills, a studio usually gets there faster without the cost of a permanent team.

Is a freelancer cheaper than a studio?

Per hour, often. Per project, not always. A single person covering design, backend, AI and testing takes longer, and the work stops if they're unavailable.

Can a studio work alongside our own developers?

Yes. Studios often build a piece your team doesn't have time for, or the first version before a handover.
